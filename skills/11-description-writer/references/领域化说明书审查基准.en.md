# Field-specific description review standard

## General review

- The technical problem, the technical solution, and the technical effect form a causal chain that can be checked.
- Every claim feature has specific support in the description (说明书) and the drawings. Terms stay consistent.
- A broad generalization has several embodiments, alternatives, or a general principle behind it. Do not extrapolate without limit from a single embodiment.
- Parameters, ranges, materials, step order, and effects all have a source. Endpoints and preferred ranges have a basis.
- A person skilled in the art can carry the invention out from this disclosure, without inventive effort or undue experimentation.

## Machines

- State part shapes, relative positions, how parts connect or fit, assembly relationships, motion constraints, and the working process.
- Where a dimension relationship, clearance, angle, or material is required for the effect, state the basis. Where it is not required, give a reasonable alternative.
- States, sections, and exploded relationships in the figures can be restored in the text to the real assembled structure.

## Electrical and control systems

- State module functions, ports, signal direction, power and ground, data or control relationships, and operating timing.
- Writing only "the module is used to achieve a function" is usually not enough. State the connections, processing logic, or key conditions that make the function possible.
- Hardware, software, and the control flow should correspond.

## Software and algorithms

- State the actor that performs the steps, the input data, the processing steps, the key data structures or models, the output, and the relationship to hardware or to a concrete technical setting.
- The technical effect should come from a concrete technical means. Do not substitute commercial revenue, management convenience, or abstract algorithm performance.
- For key model training, parameters, rules, or flows, give enough disclosure to carry them out, and avoid writing a non-essential implementation detail as the only way.

## Methods and processes

- State who or what performs each step, order and parallel relationships, conditional branches, inputs and outputs, process conditions, and exception handling.
- Explain why the steps together solve the technical problem. Do not merely list unrelated operations.
- When a range, order, or condition can vary, state the boundary of that variation and its effect.

## Chemical technology

- Check compound names, structural formulas, substituents, stereochemistry, starting materials, reaction conditions, purification, characterization, and yield.
- A broad general formula or a numerical range needs enough representative embodiments and an effect that can reasonably be expected.
- Do not generate experimental data, comparative examples, or effects. If they are missing, ask the inventor to supply them.

## Biological technology

- State sequences, vectors, hosts, samples, reagents, experimental conditions, detection methods, statistics or functional validation, and what is needed to repeat the work.
- When the case involves sequences, a deposit, genetic resources, or source disclosure, list those compliance checks separately and leave them for a practicing patent attorney to confirm.
- When a diagnostic, therapeutic, or use subject has a subject-matter risk, do not avoid it merely by changing the title. Return to the technical facts and the claim strategy.

## Mixed inventions

- Meet the disclosure requirements of each relevant field, and use one interface table to explain how the mechanical, electrical, software, process, or chemical/biological parts work together.
- Do not let chapters for different fields use inconsistent names for parts, modules, steps, or effects.
